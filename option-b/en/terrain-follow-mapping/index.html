Terrain-follow drone mapping for hilly and steep sites

Terrain-follow mapping is a mapping flight in which the drone holds a constant height above the ground rather than above its take-off point. Over slopes, wadis, mountain roads and quarry faces, every image is captured from the same distance to the ground. The result is even resolution, consistent overlap and a cleaner elevation model.

Why it matters on hilly ground

At a fixed height above take-off, the drone is too close to the ground on the hilltop and too far from it in the wadi. That causes:

  • Uneven resolution: some parts of the map are sharp and others blurred.
  • Inconsistent overlap: high areas get less overlap, leaving holes in the model.
  • Safety margins: too little clearance from ground and obstacles at the high points.

With terrain following, the whole site is captured at the same GSD and the model is more uniform end to end.

How it works

  1. Plan the flight on an elevation model of the area, or with the drone's sensors [TO FILL: terrain-follow method supported by DroneAtlas's equipment].
  2. Set the height above ground for the required resolution, with a safety margin from obstacles.
  3. Fly a planned route with high overlap, then process as usual in WebODM (OpenDroneMap).
  4. Deliver the same outputs as geodetic mapping: orthophoto, DSM and DTM, contours and point cloud.

When to use it

  • Quarries and pits with faces and large height differences. See volume surveys.
  • Roads and infrastructure in hilly terrain, corridors along slopes.
  • Hillside construction sites and earthworks on sloping ground. See construction progress mapping.
  • Open land with hills and gullies.

Terrain-follow FAQ

Does every site need terrain following?

No. Fairly flat ground is fine with a standard flight. We recommend it when the height range is large relative to the flight height.

Are the deliverables different?

The deliverables are the same: orthophoto, elevation models, contours and point cloud. The difference is their quality and consistency.

What about ground control on hilly sites?

On hilly ground, ground control points should cover both high and low areas, so the model is anchored across the full height range.
