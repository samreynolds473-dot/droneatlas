Ground control point (GCP) placement and survey

Ground control points (GCPs) are targets marked on the ground whose positions are measured with a GNSS receiver and used to tie the mapping model to the site. DroneAtlas plans the layout, marks and measures the points, and adds separate check points to test the result. You can order it as part of geodetic mapping or on its own, for example for a surveyor who processes in-house.

Why ground control, even with RTK

RTK gives each image a precise position, but control and check points are how you know for sure that the model sits correctly on the ground, especially in height. When the deliverable is used for quantities, design or as a surveyor's base, this is the check that gives you confidence in the numbers.

What the service includes

  • Layout planning based on site size, terrain and the purpose of the mapping.
  • Target marking, permanent or temporary, clearly visible from the air.
  • GNSS measurement [TO FILL: receiver model and method, e.g. network RTK].
  • Separate check points kept out of processing, for an independent accuracy check.
  • A point file (name, X, Y, Z on the Israeli Grid) and a photo of each target.
  • Integration into processing in WebODM (OpenDroneMap) and an error report (RMS) against the check points.

How many points?

It depends on site size, shape, height range and the accuracy needed. A long or hilly site needs a layout that covers the edges and the different heights. The number and placement are set during planning and stated in the proposal.

For surveyors

If you mark and measure the points yourself, we fly and process to your points. If you want us to do it, we hand you the point file and photos. See drone data for surveyors.

GCP FAQ

What is the difference between a control point and a check point?

A control point is used to anchor the model during processing. A check point is kept out of processing and used only to test how close the model is to reality.

Can targets stay for later rounds?

Yes. On sites with repeat rounds, permanent targets mean every round is tied to the same points.
