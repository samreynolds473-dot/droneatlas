Construction progress mapping for contractors and developers

Construction progress mapping is a series of flights on the same route over the site. Each flight produces a set: an orthophoto or a 3D model, images from fixed viewpoints, and earthwork quantities when ordered.

  • Orthophoto
  • Quantities
  • Same route
  • Quoted as a series

It is DroneAtlas's core service, built for mid-size contractors and developers across Israel. Comparison between rounds holds only when the route and the control stay the same. The proposal covers the series, not only the first flight.

01

Who it is for

  • Developer and project manager one picture everyone sees: the site on a date.
  • Supervisor a gap between design and what was built, if there is a georeferenced design file.
  • Lender or owner a dated record that is hard to argue with when the conversation is about programme and quantities.
  • The earthworks contractor on site cut and fill. See volume calculation.

02

How it works

  1. Define the site boundary, what each round includes, and the frequency: weekly, fortnightly, monthly, or by milestone.
  2. Lock a route and fixed photo viewpoints. At the start we record existing conditions and a baseline model.
  3. Plan the flight Route, altitude and camera angles matched to the deliverable.
  4. Fly and deliver after every round: a map, photos, and quantities if they were ordered.
  5. Compare with the previous round and with the design, when a georeferenced DWG or SHP is provided.

04

Accuracy and quality control

RTK gives every image a position at capture. Ground control points tie the model to the site, and separate check points, kept out of processing, test it. The accuracy and the check method are set in the proposal for the purpose of the job and written into the quality-control report. Where check points were surveyed, the report includes the error against them (RMS). Typical accuracy: [TO FILL: horizontal and vertical accuracy actually measured with DroneAtlas's setup. Do not publish a figure before testing].

Resolution (GSD) is the ground area one pixel represents. Accuracy is how close a map position is to the true position. High resolution does not guarantee high accuracy, so the two are defined separately. Processing is done in WebODM (OpenDroneMap). The default coordinate system is the Israeli Grid (EPSG:2039), or another system on request.

05

When to use it, and when to choose something else

  • One visit and no follow-up a single geodetic survey is enough when there is nothing to compare.
  • Only a film for a meeting industrial photography without a measurable model.
  • Monthly quarry inventory a volume survey with a fixed base surface.
  • A permit map is signed by a partner licensed surveyor. The mapping here records construction.

06

Limits and approvals

The service covers all of Israel, subject to flight approvals and airspace limits at the site. Commercial operation of a small drone requires a small-UAS pilot licence from the Civil Aviation Authority [TO FILL: licence number] and a registered aircraft [TO FILL: registration numbers]. In a built-up area, or when flying over people, vehicles or infrastructure, we need the site owner's written consent and sometimes a special authorisation. We coordinate in advance with the person in charge on site.

Strong wind, rain or fog delays the flight, and we set a nearby alternative date. Capture is planned to stay on the commissioned area, and we do not publish material that identifies people or third-party property. On an active site the flight is planned so it does not pass over workers and operating cranes, and so it follows Civil Aviation Authority rules. On an urban site, flying over people, vehicles or infrastructure needs written consent and sometimes a special authorisation. We check in advance and apply when needed.

07

What to prepare

  • The site location, a map pin or a boundary file (KML, KMZ, DWG, DXF, SHP or GeoJSON).
  • Approximate size, the purpose, the deliverables you want and a preferred date.
  • An on-site contact, and access on the flight day.
  • If you have them: a georeferenced design file (DWG or SHP) or a previous survey.
  • The frequency you want, and who on the team should receive the link.

08

FAQ

How often should the site be flown?

It depends on the project pace and what needs reporting. Some projects fly monthly and others at each milestone. We match the frequency to the site.

Can conditions be compared with the drawings?

Yes. If you provide a georeferenced design file (DWG or SHP), we overlay it on the orthophoto and mark deviations.

Who gets access to the deliverables?

You decide. We can share a link with every stakeholder or deliver the files to you only.
