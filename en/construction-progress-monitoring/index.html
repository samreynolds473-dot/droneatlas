Construction progress mapping for contractors and developers

Drone construction progress mapping is a series of repeat flights on the same route over your site. Each flight produces a mapping set: a site orthophoto or 3D model, images from fixed viewpoints, and earthwork quantities. That gives you consistent records across the project that you can compare with the design and between rounds. It is DroneAtlas's core service, built for mid-size contractors and developers across Israel.

Frequency: weekly, fortnightly or monthly, depending on the pace of the project. We quote the whole series, not just the first flight, because the same route and control are what make comparisons reliable.

What you receive each round

  • An up-to-date orthophoto of the site on the Israeli Grid. See orthophoto and aerial mapping.
  • Photos and video from fixed viewpoints, so each round is directly comparable.
  • A 3D model where needed. See 3D models.
  • Quantities for cut, fill and stockpiles. See volume calculation.
  • Design comparison: the design layer overlaid on the orthophoto to spot deviations.
  • A dated archive that is easy to share with every stakeholder.

How it helps the project

  • One picture for the whole team: developer, project manager, supervisor and lender see the same conditions.
  • Fewer site visits just to understand what is happening.
  • Records that are hard to dispute: dated conditions, useful in discussions about schedules and quantities.
  • Early detection of gaps between design and execution.

Building a monitoring plan

  1. Define the site boundary, the deliverables per round and the frequency.
  2. Plan a fixed flight route and fixed photo viewpoints.
  3. Check airspace and site coordination, including work near cranes and people.
  4. Fly each round on schedule and deliver after every round.

Safety on an active site

Flights are coordinated with the site manager. They are planned to avoid passing over workers and operating cranes, and to comply with Civil Aviation Authority rules. On urban sites, flying over people, vehicles or infrastructure needs the site owner's written consent and sometimes a special CAAI authorisation. We check this in advance and apply when needed.

Construction monitoring FAQ

How often should the site be flown?

It depends on the project pace and what needs reporting. Some projects fly monthly and others at each milestone. We match the frequency to the site.

Can conditions be compared with the drawings?

Yes. If you provide a georeferenced design file (DWG or SHP), we overlay it on the orthophoto and mark deviations.

Who gets access to the deliverables?

You decide. We can share a link with every stakeholder or deliver the files to you only.
