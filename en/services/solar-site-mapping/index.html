Services

Drones for solar sites

From bare land to an operating farm.

From bare groundto a working array.

During development, an aerial survey and terrain model describe slope, aspect and drainage, which the layout design relies on. After construction, georeferenced mapping documents the arrays, access roads and infrastructure as one current base map.

How it works

  1. 1

    Assess the site

    A survey of the candidate land shows slopes, aspects and drainage channels before the investment.

  2. 2

    Follow construction

    Flights during the works record progress on the arrays, roads and cabling.

  3. 3

    Record the final state

    After construction, a georeferenced base map of the operating field supports operation and maintenance.

What you receive

  1. 01

    Terrain model and contours

    Slope, aspect and drainage for layout design

  2. 02

    Site orthophoto

    A georeferenced base map for developer and contractor

  3. 03

    Infrastructure documentation

    Arrays, roads and cable routes, during and after construction

Who it is for

  • Developers assessing candidate sites
  • EPC contractors in design and construction
  • Operators who need a current record of the farm

Drone Atlas is not a licensed surveyor. The deliverables are for design, control and quantities. A statutory survey plan, such as a permit map or a boundary determination, is signed by a partner licensed surveyor.

FAQ

Questions about solar site mapping

What does a thermal panel inspection look for?

Panels or cells hotter than their surroundings. A hot spot can indicate a fault in the panel, a connection or a diode. Every finding is marked on the site map.

Can you map before the farm is built?

Yes. Before construction we map the land for layout design: topography, slopes and drainage. On an operating farm we move to thermal inspections and records.

Does production need to stop during the flight?

No. Thermal inspection is done while the farm is operating, because only then are the heat differences visible.
