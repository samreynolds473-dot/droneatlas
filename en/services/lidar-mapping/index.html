Drone LiDAR mapping

The ground, even under the trees.

The ground itself,even under the trees.

LiDAR is airborne laser scanning (Light Detection and Ranging). The scanner measures a very large number of points and builds a dense, accurate point cloud. Because laser pulses pass through gaps in vegetation, LiDAR can capture the ground beneath trees, where photography sees only the canopy.

How it works

  1. 1

    Scan with laser

    The LiDAR scanner sends laser pulses and measures the returns, from the canopy and from the ground beneath it.

  2. 2

    Classify the cloud

    The point cloud is split into ground, vegetation, buildings and lines, each in its own layer.

  3. 3

    Produce deliverables

    A ground model, sections and contours are produced from the classified cloud, even in covered areas.

What you receive

  1. 01

    Classified point cloud

    LAS or LAZ, with ground separated from vegetation

  2. 02

    DTM beneath vegetation

    The ground model, including covered areas

  3. 03

    Contours and sections

    DXF/DWG or SHP

Who it is for

  • Areas with dense vegetation
  • Infrastructure corridors: power lines, pipelines and roads
  • Forests and complex terrain

Drone Atlas is not a licensed surveyor. The deliverables are for design, control and quantities. A statutory survey plan, such as a permit map or a boundary determination, is signed by a partner licensed surveyor.

Tailored to the client

The same service serves different clients in different ways. This is how it looks for each of them.

  • Municipalities & infrastructure

    City infrastructure, even under the trees.

    In a local authority, much of what needs mapping is hidden: pavements and paths under street trees, drainage channels inside vegetation, and power and lighting lines running above the road. A LiDAR scan captures both the ground beneath the canopy and the lines and poles above it, producing one model of the street strip or infrastructure route for planning, maintenance and clearance checks.

    View client page

  • Nature reserves & agriculture

    The ground under the forest, and the forest structure above it.

    In forest and scrub, photography sees only the canopy. A LiDAR scan passes through gaps in the vegetation and produces both the ground surface, with paths, channels and terraces that cannot be seen from above, and the height and density of the trees. The data supports planning of firebreaks and access tracks, forest management and tracking change.

    View client page

  • Surveyors & planners

    A classified point cloud, for ground the camera cannot see.

    Where the ground is covered by vegetation, photogrammetry gives the height of the canopy, not the ground. A LiDAR scan produces a dense point cloud in which ground is separated from vegetation and buildings, so a reliable DTM, sections and contours can be derived even in scrub, in a wadi or along a route.

FAQ

Questions about liDAR mapping

When do I need LiDAR rather than photos?

When the ground is hidden by vegetation. Laser pulses pass between the leaves and reach the ground, so LiDAR suits forests, wooded corridors and densely vegetated areas.

What does a LiDAR scan deliver?

A classified point cloud (ground, vegetation, structures) and a terrain model built from the ground points only.

Can LiDAR be combined with photos?

Yes. They are often combined: LiDAR for geometry under vegetation, photos for the orthophoto and texture.
